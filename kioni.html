Summary

By the time a performance review comes around, most people have forgotten what they achieved months earlier. Kioni already let people save examples of their work and turn them into a report, but it felt like a form to fill in, not something you'd come back to every Friday. I redesigned the whole product around one calm weekly habit, made every consequence clear before people act, and designed every screen in light mode, dark mode and on phone.

Audit & Research

The product worked. It just felt like a form.

I started with the live product, not a blank page. I went through every screen, studied how each feature behaves, and reviewed five competitors in two groups.

PERSONAL WIN TRACKERS

BragLog, BragBook. Help you note wins and turn them into review documents, but they're for you alone, with no link to your manager.

COMPANY PERFORMANCE TOOLS

Lattice, 15Five, Leapsome. Powerful for goals and reviews, but owned by the company, not the employee.

The biggest rival is free: a Google Doc or Notion template. Kioni has to feel better than a blank document.

Where Kioni fits: private like a personal diary, but it can still connect to your manager, and you choose exactly what they see.

  1. The idea was strong. Saving small examples of real work, turning chosen ones into a report, and a weekly growth loop (choose an area, try an action, record the result, look back) are genuinely useful.
  2. The experience was flat. Long single-column pages, generic styling, and a settings page with seven separate Save buttons made it feel like admin, not a personal notebook.
  3. The rules were good but hidden. Free plan limits, what happens when you edit an example that's already in a report, and how private everything is were all real features, but users only found out after they acted.

"The hardest part of this kind of product is remembering to log your wins. So the redesign makes the Friday check-in a core part of Home, not a setting hidden away."

Key Decisions

Key decisions

Five principles decided every screen

When two options looked equally good, these decided it.

  1. Private by default. Nothing leaves Kioni unless you copy, print or download it. Every screen says who can see what.
  2. Your words, kept as you wrote them. Anything you write about yourself sits on cream "paper". Kioni never rewrites it, and edits keep a history.
  3. Calm, not gamified. No scores, no streak shaming, no red badges. Every result counts, including "Not yet".
  4. Small steps, real work. Thirty seconds on a Friday is enough. One question at a time, and anything extra is marked optional.
  5. Honest by design. Limits, prices and consequences are shown before you act, never after.

Being honest about consequences builds trust

People trust Kioni with private notes about their work. If it surprises them, they stop trusting it.

  • Delete says exactly what will happen: "This example and its 2 earlier versions will be removed. This can't be undone. Your report will ask you to refresh."
  • Editing an example flags any report that uses it. The report is marked "Needs a look", and downloading pauses until you check it, so what you share is always up to date.
  • Limits are shown before you reach them: "Free includes 1 open growth area. See plans." Calm gold notes, never red warnings.
  • AI is optional, and its button is deliberately quiet, so nobody is pushed into sharing their work with AI.

Trade-offs I made

I chose Over Because
Calm progress Scores and streaks Streak shaming makes people quit a tool meant to make them feel good
One question at a time One long form Thirty seconds is only possible if every step is tiny
Pausing downloads when an example changes Letting people download anyway A slightly slower download beats sharing an out-of-date report
A quiet, optional AI button A big AI feature Privacy comes before promoting a feature

Design Execution

The design

Your words belong on paper

The visual design separates the person from the tool:

  • White cards hold the things you use.
  • Cream paper holds what you wrote about yourself.
  • Gold appears only for wins and things that need a look.
  • Green means you can act.
  • Red is used for one thing only: delete.

Colours: Forest #1F5C45 · Deep forest #1E3A2F · Mint #86D6B2 · Gold #E3B661 · Cream #F7F3EC · Paper #FFFDF6
Fonts: Fraunces for headings and your own words · Inter for the interface · Caveat for reflections only

Settings went from 3,700px to 1,560px on phone, without losing anything

The old settings page was one long column with seven Save buttons. I:

  • grouped all reminders into one "Check-ins and reminders" section
  • asked for the time zone once
  • hid settings that do nothing, like a check-in time when check-ins are off
  • made switches save instantly, with a small "Saved" message

On phone, each section folds into a card showing its current setting in one line, so people can check everything without opening anything.

Designed for every state, theme and screen

  • Dark mode is designed, not just inverted. Every colour has a chosen dark version, and paper still looks like paper.
  • Every screen works on phone. Forms swap the menu bar for a pinned "Cancel · Save" bar.
  • Loading feels steady. The layout stays in place, and loading screens only appear after 300 milliseconds, so fast pages never flash.
  • Accessible: every colour pair meets WCAG AA, tap areas are at least 44px, focus is always visible, status never relies on colour alone, and reduced-motion settings are respected.
  • Beyond the happy path: empty, loading and unusual states are designed for every screen.

Retrospective

What I learned

In a product about people's own work, calm and clarity matter more than features. Kioni already did the right things. The redesign made them feel trustworthy and easy enough to do every week.

Honesty is cheap to design and expensive to skip. An honest delete message costs one sentence, and it earns trust a feature can't.

AI sped up the work, but the decisions were mine. I used AI tools to explore options and build the prototype quickly, which gave me more time for the small details that build trust.
